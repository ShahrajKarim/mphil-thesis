% ===== Abstract =====
%TC:ignore
\thispagestyle{empty}
\vspace*{3cm}
\begin{center}
  {\Large \underline{Abstract}}
\end{center}
\vspace{1cm}

\noindent Expansions in health insurance coverage increase the size of pharmaceutical markets and are expected to raise incentives for innovation. Yet, when the resulting demand is channelled through a public buyer that governs pricing and market access, the effect on innovation is less clear. This paper examines that tension through the lens of the 2014 Medicaid expansion under the Affordable Care Act. I construct novel firm-by-therapeutic-class panels linking patent and FDA drug approval records with Medicaid prescription and Preferred Drug List data to examine the roles of demand and Medicaid pricing exposure. Therapeutic classes with greater pre-expansion Medicaid exposure experienced larger declines in patenting after the reform, while evidence for FDA approvals is inconclusive. The patenting decline is stronger among firms more exposed to Medicaid's formulary system and in therapeutic classes where drugs are more substitutable. An illustrative model formalises this mechanism to show how public insurance expansions can reduce innovation incentives when the Medicaid rebate wedge offsets the rents generated by additional sales.
